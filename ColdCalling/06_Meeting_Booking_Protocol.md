# Meeting Booking Protocol — GTA Deli Packaging
## Scripts, Confirmation Emails & Pre-Meeting Checklists

---

## A. MEETING SCRIPT — BOOKING DURING A CALL

*Use this when a prospect sounds engaged and would benefit from seeing samples or discussing volume pricing.*

---

### When to Propose a Meeting

Signals that it's time to suggest an in-person meeting:
- They've asked about multiple product categories
- They mention a frustration with their current supplier
- They're a high-volume prospect (caterer, grocer, multi-location)
- They want to see samples before ordering
- They're the decision maker and seem genuinely interested

---

### Meeting Proposal Script

> "Based on what you've shared, I think it would make a lot of sense for me to come by in person. I can bring samples of the products you mentioned, along with our pricing sheet, so you can actually see and feel the quality difference.
>
> It would only take about 15–20 minutes of your time. Would [Tuesday] or [Wednesday] this week work for you? Morning or afternoon, whatever is easier for you."

*[Get a specific day, date, and time.]*

---

### Pre-Confirmation Questions (Ask Before Hanging Up)

Before confirming, ask these to prepare properly:

> 1. "Just to make sure I bring the right products — what are the main packaging items you go through most right now?"
>
> 2. "Roughly how often do you reorder, and what quantities do you typically need?"
>
> 3. "And you'd be the right person to make a decision on switching suppliers, or is there anyone else who should be part of that conversation?"
>
> 4. "What's the best address for me to come to?"

---

### Confirm and Close the Booking

> "Perfect. So that's [Day, Date] at [Time] at [their address]. I'll send you a quick confirmation email with all the details. And just so I have it — what's the best email for you?"

**Log in CRM immediately: Status → "Meeting Booked"**

## C. MEETING AGENDA

**Share this verbally at the start of the meeting:**

> "Thanks so much for having me. I want to be respectful of your time, so here's what I was thinking we'd cover:
>
> 1. I'll give you a quick 2-minute overview of who we are
> 2. We'll look at the samples I brought — I'll show you the items relevant to your operation
> 3. I'll walk you through our pricing
> 4. You can ask any questions
>
> Sound good? And I promise we'll be done in 20 minutes or less."

---

### Meeting Agenda — Written Version

```
[COMPANY NAME] — MEETING AGENDA

Date:      [Date & Time]
Location:  [Their Business Address]
With:      [Contact Name], [Business Name]
Rep:       [Your Name]

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. Brief Introduction (2 min)
   - Who we are, where we're based, who we serve

2. Understanding Their Needs (5 min)
   - Current packaging usage
   - Pain points with current supplier
   - Volume and ordering frequency

3. Product Samples Walkthrough (8 min)
   - Relevant products for their business
   - Quality demonstration
   - Custom branding options

4. Pricing Overview (3 min)
   - Wholesale pricing by volume tier
   - Delivery terms and minimums

5. Next Steps (2 min)
   - Trial order / quote / standing account setup

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Total: ~20 minutes
```

## D. PRE-MEETING CHECKLIST

**Prepare 24 hours before the meeting:**

### Materials to Bring
- [ ] **Product samples** — based on what they mentioned on the call (containers, vacuum bags, trays, etc.)
- [ ] **Printed price list** — current version, organized by category
- [ ] **Product catalogue** — printed, full colour, professionally bound/stapled
- [ ] **One-page sales flyer** — leave-behind
- [ ] **Introduction letter / brochure** — one copy to leave with them
- [ ] **Business cards** — at least 5
- [ ] **Quote form / notepad** — to write up a custom quote on the spot
- [ ] **Pen** — don't rely on theirs
- [ ] **Laptop or tablet (optional)** — to show website and extra product images

### Digital Preparation
- [ ] Review their **Google Maps listing / website** before going
- [ ] Know their **business type and likely pain points** (from call notes in CRM)
- [ ] Prepare a **rough quote** based on what they said they use monthly
- [ ] Have the **order form / account setup form** ready

### Personal Preparation
- [ ] **Professional appearance** — business casual minimum
- [ ] **Leave early** — arrive 5 minutes before, not late
- [ ] **Confirm the meeting** by phone or email 24 hours prior
- [ ] **Know your opening line** for when you walk in

## E. WHAT TO SAY WHEN LEAVING

### If They Want to Order:
> "That's excellent — let me get your account set up right now. Just a few quick details and we can have your first order in transit within [X] days."

*[Complete account setup form / take order details]*

### If They Want to Think About It:
> "Absolutely, no pressure. I'll leave the catalogue and pricing with you. When do you think is a good time for a quick follow-up call — would [specific day] work?"

### If They Said No (but were polite):
> "I really appreciate your time today. Here's my card — if anything ever changes with your current supplier, please keep us in mind. We're always happy to compete for your business."

### If They Want a Custom Quote:
> "Perfect. I'll put that together today and email it to you. Just to confirm — I need [quantities, specific SKUs they want, delivery address]. And you'll have it by [tomorrow / end of day]."

---

## F. POST-MEETING FOLLOW-UP

### Same day (within 2 hours):
Send a brief "thank you" email:

```
Subject: Great meeting today — [Company Name] follow-up

Hi [Name],

It was a pleasure meeting you today at [Business Name] — thank you for your time.

As promised, I'm following up with [our catalogue / the quote you requested / the sample info].

Next step: [what you agreed to — e.g., I'll have a formal quote to you by end of day / Let's connect again on Thursday]

Talk soon,
[Your Name]
[Phone]
```

### Within 3 business days:
- Call to follow up on the quote or materials
- Ask if they have any questions
- Aim to close or establish a clear next step

---

*Every meeting should end with a defined next step. Never leave without knowing when you're talking next.*

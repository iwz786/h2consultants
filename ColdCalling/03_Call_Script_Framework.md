# Complete Call Script Framework — GTA Deli Packaging
## Professional Cold Calling Scripts, Objection Handling & Closing

---

> **BEFORE EVERY CALL:** Take a breath. Smile (it comes through in your voice). Have the CRM open and ready to log. Know the business type you're calling.

---

## A. OPENING SCRIPT (First 15 Seconds)

*Goal: Get them to stay on the line. Be warm, clear, and quick.*

---

### Standard Opening (Restaurant / Deli / Caterer)

> "Hi, is this [Business Name]? Great — my name is [Your Name], I'm calling from [Company Name], we're a local packaging supplier right here in the GTA.
>
> The reason I'm reaching out is we work with a lot of [restaurants / delis / caterers] in [their area — e.g., Scarborough, Brampton], and we help them save money on their food packaging while actually getting better quality.
>
> I just have a quick question for you — do you have about 60 seconds?"

**If they say YES:** Move to Discovery Questions (Section B)
**If they say "what's this about?":** "Totally fair — I'll be quick. We supply deli containers, food trays, takeout packaging — everything a [restaurant/deli] like yours goes through every week. I just wanted to see if it makes sense to send you our pricing."

---

### Gatekeeper Version (When Receptionist / Employee Answers)

> "Hi there, my name is [Your Name] from [Company Name]. I'm hoping to speak with whoever handles your purchasing or supplies — is that the owner or a manager? … Great, could I speak with them please?"

**If they ask what it's about:** "I'm a local packaging supplier — we work with a lot of [restaurants / delis] in the area. I just want to introduce ourselves and see if our pricing might make sense for them."

---

### Callback Version (If No Answer — Leave Voicemail)

> "Hi, this message is for [Name / whoever handles purchasing] at [Business Name]. My name is [Your Name] from [Company Name] — we're a GTA-based food packaging supplier. We work with [restaurants/delis] across the area and I wanted to introduce ourselves.
>
> If packaging costs or delivery are ever a frustration, I think we might be able to help. I'll follow up in a couple of days, but feel free to reach me at [your number]. Thanks so much, have a great day."

---

## B. DISCOVERY QUESTIONS

*Goal: Understand their current situation so you can personalize your pitch. Listen more than you talk.*

---

### Question Flow (Use 3–5 of these — don't interrogate)

**1. Current supplier:**
> "Just so I understand your situation better — do you currently have a packaging supplier you're working with, or do you shop around?"

**2. Products they use:**
> "What kinds of packaging are you going through most right now — is it mostly containers, wraps, trays, bags, or a mix?"

**3. Volume:**
> "Roughly how often do you reorder? Weekly? Monthly?"

**4. Pain points:**
> "Is there anything about your current supplier that you'd change if you could — whether it's pricing, delivery, or the product itself?"

**5. Custom branding interest:**
> "Have you ever thought about having your logo on your packaging? Some of our clients do custom printing — it's more affordable than people expect."

**6. Decision making:**
> "Are you the person who handles the purchasing for supplies like this, or is there someone else involved?"

---

### Listening for Signals

| What they say | What it means |
|--------------|---------------|
| "We get it from [big chain store]" | They're not on wholesale — major cost savings opportunity |
| "Our current supplier is fine" | Dig for ANY issue — price, delivery, minimums |
| "We've been having issues with delivery" | Hot lead — address immediately |
| "We go through a LOT of containers" | High-volume buyer — push volume pricing |
| "We've thought about custom packaging" | Strong upsell opportunity |
| "My manager/boss handles that" | Ask to speak with the right person |

---

## C. VALUE PROPOSITION DELIVERY

*Goal: Connect our strengths to THEIR specific pain points.*

---

### Core Value Statement (Customize Based on Their Answers)

> "Based on what you've told me, I think we could actually be a really good fit for you. Here's why:
>
> We're a local GTA supplier — so delivery is fast, usually within [X] business days, and you're not waiting on a shipment from overseas.
>
> Everything we carry is food-grade certified, so you're fully covered from a health and safety standpoint.
>
> And because we sell wholesale, our pricing is typically 15–30% lower than what you'd find at a restaurant supply store.
>
> Plus, there's no massive minimum order — so you can start small, try us out, and if you love the product, we scale with you."

---

### Tailored Pitches by Business Type

**For Restaurants:**
> "A lot of our restaurant clients switched to us because they were tired of paying retail prices at kitchen supply stores. We set them up with a standing order so they never run out, and the delivery comes right to their door."

**For Delis:**
> "For a deli like yours, the packaging almost becomes part of the product — the presentation matters. We have great options for deli containers and wraps, and we can even put your logo on them if you want to stand out."

**For Caterers:**
> "Caterers especially love us because we can do large orders on short notice. When you've got an event coming up and you need 500 trays in three days, we're who you call."

**For Grocery / Butcher:**
> "We carry vacuum seal bags that are excellent for display — high clarity so your product always looks its best. And they seal reliably, which means fewer returns and complaints."

## D. HANDLING COMMON OBJECTIONS

---

### Objection 1: "We already have a supplier."

> **Rep:** "That's totally understandable — most businesses we work with had a supplier before they found us. Can I ask — are you completely happy with them, or is there anything you wish was a bit better? Even just the pricing or the delivery timing?"

*[They'll usually say something. Bridge from there.]*

> "Here's what I'd suggest — we're not asking you to drop your current supplier. Just let us send you our pricing sheet. Compare it side by side. If it makes sense, great. If not, no pressure. Would that be fair?"

---

### Objection 2: "We're not interested."

> **Rep:** "I completely respect that. Can I just ask — is it that you're happy with everything as it is, or is it just not a good time?"

*[If not a good time:]* "When would be a better time to reconnect? I can call back next month."

*[If happy with everything:]* "That's great, honestly. Would you mind if I sent a quick email with our pricing, just so you have it on file? That way, if anything ever changes, you know exactly who to call."

---

### Objection 3: "Send me information via email."

*Note: This is often a polite brush-off, but if followed up properly, it converts.*

> **Rep:** "Absolutely, happy to. What's the best email to send it to?"

*[Get the email.]*

> "Perfect. And just so I can make the email relevant to you — it sounds like you mainly use [what they mentioned]. Is that right? … Great. And just to confirm, you'd be the right person to follow up with after I send it?"

*[Schedule the follow-up on the spot.]*

> "I'll send that over today. I'll give you a quick call [Wednesday/in 2 days] just to make sure it landed and see if you have any questions. Does that work?"

---

### Objection 4: "Your prices are too high."

*[Only applicable if you've already quoted — otherwise ask:]*

> **Rep:** "I completely hear you. Out of curiosity, what are you paying right now for [specific product]? I want to make sure I'm comparing apples to apples."

*[If they share their price:]*
> "Okay, so here's the thing — when we factor in the delivery cost and the quality difference, a lot of clients find we're actually more affordable all-in. Can I put a specific quote together for you based on what you actually go through each month? That way you can see an exact number."

*[If they won't share:]*
> "Fair enough. Let me send you our volume pricing — at certain order levels, our pricing gets very competitive. Would you want to see that?"

---

### Objection 5: "I need to think about it."

> **Rep:** "Of course, that's totally reasonable — it's not a small decision. Can I ask, is there a specific piece of information that would help you make the decision? Pricing? Product quality? Delivery timing?"

*[Address their specific concern, then:]*

> "How about this — I'll follow up with you on [specific day]. By then you'll have had a chance to look over the email I send and maybe talk to whoever else needs to be involved. Does [day] work?"

---

### Objection 6: "I'm too busy right now."

> **Rep:** "I completely understand — I'll be quick. When's a better time for you — later today, or sometime this week?"

*[If they give a time, book it immediately.]*
> "Perfect, I'll call you [day] at [time]. I'll put it in my calendar right now. What's the best number to reach you at?"

*[If they're truly dismissive:]*
> "No worries at all. I'll send a quick email with our info so you have it, and I'll follow up in a couple of weeks. Does that work?"

---

### Objection 7: "Call me back later."

> **Rep:** "Absolutely. When would be the best time — is it better to reach you in the morning or afternoon?"

*[Get a specific callback time and date.]*

> "Great, I'll call you [day] at [time]. And just to confirm I'm calling the right number — is this your direct line, or is there a better one?"

*[Log the callback immediately. Always call back exactly when you said you would.]*

---

## E. CLOSING TECHNIQUES

---

### Soft Close — Email/Info Permission
*(Use when they're slightly warm but not ready to commit)*

> "Based on our conversation, I don't want to push anything — but I do think you'd find our pricing worth looking at. Can I send you a quick email with our product sheet and pricing? All low-pressure. You can look at it when you have a moment."

---

### Soft Close — Sample/Trial Order
*(Use when they're curious but hesitant)*

> "Here's what I suggest — place a small trial order. Get your hands on the product, see the quality for yourself. If you're not impressed, we won't ask for a second order. But I'm confident you'll want to stick with us."

---

### Meeting Close
*(Use when they're engaged and seem like a volume buyer)*

> "I'd love to bring some samples by for you to see in person — it makes a big difference when you can actually hold the product. Would [Tuesday or Wednesday] morning work for me to stop by for 15 minutes?"

---

### Assumptive Close
*(Use when the conversation has been very positive)*

> "You know what, based on what you've told me, I think [product X] would be a perfect fit for you. Let me put together a quote for a [weekly / monthly] standing order. What quantities do you typically go through?"

---

### Hard Close — Price-Lock Urgency
*(Use sparingly, only when appropriate)*

> "I do want to mention — we're running a new customer introductory discount right now, but it's only available until [end of month]. If you want to lock in that pricing, I'd need to get your first order in this week. Should I set that up for you?"

---

### Booking the Next Step (Always End with This)
*Never end a call without a next step committed.*

> "Great — so what I'll do is [send you our pricing / prepare a quote / book that meeting]. And I'll follow up with you on [specific day] at [specific time]. Does that work for you?"

---

## F. FOLLOW-UP PROTOCOL

---

### Follow-Up Call 2 (2–3 days after initial contact or email)

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from [Company Name] — we spoke [day]. I just wanted to make sure you got the email I sent over with our product sheet. Did you have a chance to take a quick look?"

*[If yes:]*
> "Great — did anything stand out? Or any questions I can answer for you?"

*[If no:]*
> "No worries — I'll resend it right now while I have you on the line. Let me confirm your email address is still [email]. Should take about 30 seconds to land. While I have you — did you want me to walk you through the key points quickly?"

---

### Follow-Up Call 3 (1 week later — if no response to call 2)

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from [Company Name] again. I know you're busy and I won't take much of your time. I just wanted to reach out one more time because I genuinely think our pricing and delivery could help [their business].
>
> If it's just not the right time, no problem at all — I can put you on our quarterly update list instead. But if there's any interest, I'm happy to put a specific quote together for you today. What would you prefer?"

---

### Re-Engagement for Cold Leads (30–60 days later)

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from [Company Name]. We spoke back in [month] about your packaging needs. I'm reaching out again because we just got in some new product lines [or: we have a spring pricing promotion] and I thought of you.
>
> Has anything changed on your end with your current supplier? I'd love to reconnect and see if the timing is better now."

---

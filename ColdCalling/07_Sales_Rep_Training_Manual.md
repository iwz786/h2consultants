## A. PRODUCT KNOWLEDGE

---

### Product Category 1: Deli Containers

**What they are:** Rigid plastic containers used to package deli meats, salads, cheese, prepared foods, soups, and more. Come in round and rectangular shapes with matching lids.

**Common sizes and uses:**

| Size | Common Use |
|------|-----------|
| 8 oz round | Deli salad portions, olives, spreads |
| 12 oz round | Larger salad portions, side dishes |
| 16 oz rectangular | Full portions, entrées, grain bowls |
| 32 oz rectangular | Large portions, family sizes, bulk items |

**Materials:**
- **PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate):** Crystal clear, recyclable, most common for cold deli items
- **PP (Polypropylene):** Microwave-safe, more heat-resistant, slightly cloudy
- **Black base / clear lid:** Upscale restaurant presentation

**Key selling points:**
- Crystal clear lids let customers see the product (increases perceived freshness)
- Leak-resistant — critical for soups, dressings, wet salads
- Stackable — saves shelf space in walk-in coolers
- BPA-free — important for health-conscious buyers

**Common customer objections on product:**
- "The lids pop off" → Our snap-lock designs secure firmly
- "They crack in the freezer" → Our freezer-safe PP options address this
- "I need microwave-safe containers" → Direct them to PP line

### Product Category 2: Sandwich Wraps & Deli Paper

**What they are:** Sheet and roll papers used to wrap sandwiches, deli meats, fish, baked goods, and other fresh foods.

**Types:**

| Type | Best For |
|------|---------|
| Dry wax paper | Cold deli items, sandwiches |
| Greaseproof paper | Hot/greasy items, burgers |
| Butcher paper (white or kraft) | Meat wrapping, rustic presentation |
| Foil wrap | Hot sandwiches, heat retention |
| Checkered deli paper | Classic deli counter presentation |
| Parchment paper | Baking, non-stick applications |

**Sizes:** Come in pre-cut sheets (various dimensions) or rolls (various widths)

**Key selling points:**
- Food-safe direct contact (important compliance point)
- Moisture-resistant options available
- Custom printing available on rolls (logo/design)
- Affordable per-unit cost at case quantities

---

### Product Category 3: Food Trays & Clamshells

**Clamshells:**
- Hinged, one-piece container — no separate lid needed
- Sizes: 6x6, 9x6, 9x9 (inches)
- Best for: burgers, sandwiches, appetizers, meal combos
- Options: clear plastic, black plastic, kraft (eco)

**Foam Trays:**
- Traditional white foam meat/produce trays
- Sizes: #1, #2, #3, #4, #8 (standard supermarket sizing)
- Best for: meat display, produce packaging, butcher shops
- Note: Some municipalities moving away from foam — know your customer's stance

**Aluminium Pans:**
- Half-pan and full-pan (standard catering sizes)
- Oven-safe to 400°F
- Best for: catering, meal transport, institutional food service
- Come with cardboard lids (standard) or foil lids

**Key selling points:**
- Wide size range covers all use cases
- Disposable convenience for catering and events
- Clear options maximize visual appeal of the food

---

### Product Category 4: Vacuum Seal Bags

**What they are:** Multi-layer barrier bags that, when combined with a vacuum sealer, remove air from the package to extend shelf life.

**How vacuum sealing extends shelf life:**
- Normal packaging: bacteria need oxygen to grow
- Vacuum-sealed: oxygen removed → bacteria growth slows dramatically
- Result: 3–5x longer shelf life vs. standard packaging

**Types:**

| Type | Best For |
|------|---------|
| Embossed/channel pouches | Home/commercial vacuum sealers |
| Smooth pouches | Chamber vacuum machines (common in delis/butchers) |
| Rolls | Custom-size cutting for irregular items |

**Common sizes:** 4", 6", 8", 10", 12", 15" wide; variable length pouches or continuous rolls

**Key selling points:**
- Reduces food waste significantly (cost savings for customer)
- Crystal clear — product is fully visible
- Freezer-safe — no freezer burn
- Professional, retail-ready appearance
- Tamper-evident once sealed

**Who buys them most:** Butcher shops, delis (sliced meats/cheeses), meal prep services, fish/seafood vendors, specialty food producers

### Product Category 5: Eco-Friendly Packaging

**Market context:** Growing consumer demand for sustainable packaging. City of Toronto green bin program. Many restaurants and cafes use eco packaging as a marketing differentiator.

**Types:**

| Product | Material | Key Feature |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Kraft containers | Recycled paperboard | Grease-resistant, recyclable |
| Bagasse (sugarcane fiber) | Agricultural waste | Compostable, microwave-safe |
| PLA containers | Corn starch-derived plastic | Looks like plastic, compostable |
| Compostable cutlery | CPLA or wood | Replaces single-use plastic cutlery |
| Kraft paper bags | Recycled kraft paper | Recyclable, natural look |

**Key certifications to mention:**
- BPI Certified Compostable (bagasse and PLA)
- FSC Certified Paper (for kraft products)

**Key selling points:**
- Meets City of Toronto's packaging guidelines
- Strong marketing angle for restaurants targeting eco-conscious clients
- Premium, natural aesthetic
- Increasingly cost-competitive

---

### Product Category 6: Custom Printed Packaging

**What it is:** Any standard packaging product with the customer's logo, brand colours, or custom design printed on it.

**What can be customized:**
- Deli container lids (sticker label or printed lid film)
- Paper bags (1 or 2 colour flexo printing)
- Deli wrap rolls (full custom design)
- Vacuum seal bags (full colour, custom layout)
- Custom labels/stickers (applied to any container)

**Minimum order quantities:** Vary by product — always get current MOQ from management before quoting

**Turnaround:** Typically 2–3 weeks from design approval; custom labels can be faster

**Selling this:**
> "Have you ever thought about having your logo on your packaging? It's actually more affordable than most businesses think, and it turns every meal your customer takes home into a walking advertisement for you."

## B. SALES TECHNIQUES

---

### Building Rapport Over the Phone

The first 30 seconds determine whether a prospect keeps listening.

**Do:**
- Smile — it genuinely affects your tone
- Use their name early
- Match their energy level (if they're rushed, be brief; if they're chatty, connect)
- Show genuine curiosity about their business
- Reference something specific about their business ("I noticed you're in Scarborough — we deliver there weekly")

**Don't:**
- Read a script robotically
- Rush through your intro
- Use jargon ("synergies", "value-add solution") — plain language only
- Ask "Is this a good time?" — it invites rejection; say "I'll be quick" instead

---

### Active Listening

Most salespeople talk too much. The best ones listen.

**Techniques:**
- **Echo back:** Repeat the last 2–3 words they said with a questioning tone
  - Them: "Our current supplier is inconsistent..."
  - You: "Inconsistent — in what way?"
- **Pause:** After they finish speaking, wait 2 seconds before responding. They'll often add more.
- **Confirm:** "So if I'm hearing you right, the main issue is [X] — is that correct?"
- **Take notes:** It proves you're listening and you'll remember details

---

### Tone & Pace Guidance

| Situation | Tone | Pace |
|-----------|------|------|
| Opening intro | Warm, confident | Medium |
| Asking discovery questions | Curious, genuine | Slow — let them answer |
| Presenting your USPs | Energetic, clear | Medium |
| Handling objections | Calm, empathetic | Slow |
| Closing | Confident, decisive | Medium |

**Volume:** Speak slightly louder than normal — phone compresses your voice.

**Pitch:** Avoid rising intonation at end of sentences (sounds uncertain). Statements end flat or descending.

---

### Confidence Builders

- **Reframe rejection:** "No" is not failure — it's data. Every "no" moves you closer to a "yes."
- **Know your product:** Confidence comes from knowledge. Review the product section daily in week 1.
- **Track your wins:** Write down every small win — a good conversation, an email from a prospect, a meeting booked.
- **Call streaks:** Do 10 calls in a row without stopping. Momentum overcomes anxiety.
- **Role-play regularly:** Practice objections with your manager weekly.

---

### Dealing With Rejection

You will hear "no" many times per day. This is normal and expected.

**Mindset shift:** You are not a salesperson — you are a problem solver. You are trying to find businesses that have a packaging problem you can solve. When they say no, they just don't have the problem right now. That's okay.

**What to do after a rejection:**
1. Thank them politely
2. Ask if you can follow up in 60–90 days
3. Log the call in CRM
4. Take a sip of water
5. Dial the next number immediately — don't dwell

### How to Batch Tasks

- **Group callbacks by city/area:** Call all Brampton leads together (easier mental context)
- **Group by business type:** Call all restaurants in one block
- **Batch email sends:** Write and send all follow-up emails in one 30-minute block
- **Research in dedicated blocks:** Prospect research before 9 AM or during lunch — not during calling hours

---

### Prioritizing Hot Leads

Every morning, identify your top 5 priority calls:
1. **Meetings confirmed upcoming** — confirm 24 hrs before
2. **Quotes sent 2–3 days ago** — time to follow up
3. **Promised callbacks** — call at the exact time you said you would
4. **"Interested" status leads** — follow up within 48 hours
5. **New warm leads** — anyone who responded to email or asked to be called

## D. COMPLIANCE & PROFESSIONALISM

---

### Canada's National Do Not Call List (DNCL)

**Legal requirement:** All telemarketers in Canada must respect the National Do Not Call List.

- Register with the CRTC's DNCL as a registered telemarketer (**mandatory**)
- Download and scrub your call list against the DNCL before dialing
- URL: **lnnte-dncl.gc.ca**
- Cost: Small fee based on area code blocks

**Internal DNC:** If anyone asks NOT to be called again — add them to your internal "Do Not Call" list **immediately** and do not contact them again.

---

### Professional Phone Etiquette

- Always identify yourself and your company **immediately**
- Never misrepresent who you are or why you're calling
- Do not call outside of permitted hours: **9 AM – 9 PM** (local time), Mon–Fri; and 9 AM – 6 PM on permitted days
- Do not call residential numbers unless invited
- Always be respectful, even with rude prospects

---

### Email Best Practices

- Always include your full name, company, and contact info in your signature
- Never send unsolicited email to purchased lists without CAN-SPAM/CASL compliance
- **CASL (Canadian Anti-Spam Legislation):** You must have express or implied consent to email commercially. Cold email requires implied consent (business relationship) or expressed consent.
- Include an unsubscribe option in all bulk emails
- Do not mislead subjects or senders

---

### Privacy & Data Handling

- Store prospect data only in approved systems (company CRM, your spreadsheet)
- Do not share customer information with third parties
- Do not upload prospect lists to consumer apps
- If a prospect asks what data you have on them — tell them honestly and offer to remove them

---

### Representing the Brand

You are the face of the company. Every call, email, and visit reflects on the brand.

- **Always be honest** — never overpromise on pricing, delivery, or product quality
- If you don't know an answer, say: "I'll find out and get back to you today"
- Never badmouth a competitor — focus on your strengths only
- When something goes wrong (wrong product, missed delivery), notify your manager immediately and get in front of the customer proactively
